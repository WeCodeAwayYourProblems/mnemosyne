# Lich Musings — 2026-10-16

*Working notes on the lich, the pruners, and the world's cosmology. The faceless one from the Mnemosyne prologue and the lich are the same person.*

---

## The core idea

The world is still in its infancy. Magic is hostile, explosive, and cursed because the world hasn't grown up yet. Every so often, cosmic beings called the pruners reset the world back to zero. One man has survived every reset. He wants to understand why the world is the way it is. He is the faceless one, and he is the lich.

---

## Who he is

**Settled**
- Before he became the lich, he was an ordinary person with friends, living in a world that ended.
- He didn't choose to become a lich. He was trying to stop a reset by attacking the pruners directly with magic. Becoming a lich was a side effect of that magic, a natural consequence like any other Domination effect.
- The pruners never noticed his attack. Neither they nor the planet cursed him. It was cause and effect, like punching a wall. His actions only broke his knuckles ... for eternity.
- He believes the pruners persecuted him. He's angry at them. That anger is misplaced, and it is the lock on his cage.
- He is invincible. Over centuries and millennia of watching, he has become part of the world.
- **The planet is his phylactery.** That's how he survives resets: the pruners reset the world's surface, not the world itself.
- He wants to understand why the world is so hostile to life. That question, and grief for his friends, are what keep him going.

**Proposed**
- His lichdom may have *been* the death that comes from going back to Domination after passing the Influence ceiling. He survived because the death couldn't finish with someone already half-gone.

---

## His curse

**Settled**
- He is permanently a viewer, not an actor. Anything he does in the world has to happen indirectly, through objects he makes or ideas he puts into the world, such as books.
- The cage is his mind. He believes he must dominate, but he can't, so he influences because he has to.
- For eons he has used Influence out of necessity. The moment he chooses it, everything clicks into place.
- His circumstances on the last page are the same as in the first eon. Only his reason changes.

**Proposed**
- His emotional arc and his magical arc are the same arc. Anger (the world wronged me and must be beaten) is Domination thinking. Acceptance (no one did this to me) is Influence thinking. He can't choose Influence while he still believes he was persecuted.
- What tips him from "have to" to "choose to": someone asks him something, and for the first time he wants to answer.

## The faceless one (as he appears in Mnemosyne)

From the prologue of chapter 1:
- "The faceless one had foreseen the end of the world perfectly again." The word *again* carries the whole cosmology.
- He holds a gold instrument studded with blue crystals in his ivory hand. It whirs, slows, and stills as the world ends.
- "He always returned." He calculates the interval before his next revival.
- "He raised a hand, but let it fall. Intercessions were useless." This is his curse, shown on the page.

Other appearances in the draft:
- The Mnemosyne shows Matthis visions of the faceless one: on a mountain peak while the sky burns, on a balcony while the sea dries and the mountains melt, watching people scream as the earth cracks and the sun goes out.
- The Wood Elves see those pages and say: "This Faceless One. It is much like stories of the Old Walker." That's one of his many names.
- The white room in the mine (bowl-shaped, no seams, time behaves wrong, instruments with no clear purpose) is his.

## Names and how the world knows him

**Settled**
- He is sometimes known in the world, sometimes not.
- He goes by different names in different eras: the lich, the faceless one, the Old Walker (to the Wood Elves), and others.
- Some civilizations think he's a myth. Some have gone to his castle. Some pray to him because they know there's something unique about him.
- The reveal that the prologue's figure is the lich comes later.

**Proposed**
- People pray to him, and he can't answer a single prayer. That's a fitting punishment for someone who once tried to overpower the cosmos, and it keeps him from becoming a god.

---

## The castle

**Settled**
- His castle is a physical representation of his mind: grand, full of mystery and secrets.
- It is there after every reset, because he's a feature of the planet.
- His physical body is housed in a room inside the castle. People can only interact with him one-on-one, and only if they find that room.

**Proposed**
- The castle changes as he does. Early on it is fortified, full of rooms built from anger. Later, corridors open and light gets in.
- Rooms hold his memories: archives of past resets, a room for each friend, and one locked door that no visitor ever opens, the moment he attacked the pruners.
- The white room in the mine might be a room of the castle, or the castle reaching out.

---

## His body

**Settled**
- How his body looks depends on how long it has been since the last reset. The more advanced the society around him, the more skeletal he is.
- In some eras he can't speak at all. He might use his skeletal body to hand someone a book, or *the* book.

**Proposed**
- Tie the decay to *control* rather than technology. His body withers as the world is codified, dominated, and pushed toward stagnation. That makes his body a reset clock. A late-series clue: someone finds him with more flesh than his era should allow, because that society uses Influence.
- His body can only *offer*. It can't take, strike, hold someone back, or cast. Every encounter is a small act of asking.
- Keep the book singular, with no copies. If he hands out anything, it's loose pages that always find their way back.

---

## The book (Mnemosyne)

**Settled**
- The book is the through-line, a little like the diary in Harry Potter 2, but less sentient and more cryptic and Lovecraftian.
- It appears places it shouldn't be, and characters don't realize what's happening. That's dramatic irony.
- It holds records of people who actually lived, written by them and absorbed by the lich because those records uncovered something important about the world.
- It should give the impression that the reader could be reading the book themselves.
- **How it moves:** the lich wills it to appear, but he can't control when, where, or who gets it. It is his one link to the world.

**In the draft so far**
- Blank until questioned. It answers in images, which matters because Matthis can't read.
- It keeps ending up in Matthis's hands. His satchel is heavier than it should be. He keeps "forgetting" he picked it up.

**Proposed**
- The world decides where the book lands. Every person who finds it was chosen by the world, so the infant world is quietly learning to answer a request.
- The POV side chapters (Mercy, Elspeth) could be presented as *entries* the book absorbed.

## The pruners

**Settled**
- Godlike entities, like robots, that reset the world to zero when it becomes stale. They are not malicious.
- A cosmic horror element: forces of nature, without form or knowability. They can't be fought.
- Physically they are inert until it's time to prune. They can be excavated by accident, for example by miners.
- In one scene, a trader recognizes one, the miners rebury it, and everyone is horrified because magic doesn't behave the same way near it. They're just unsettling.
- They are in this world but not part of it. Their magic comes from another, mature world. They were sent to monitor this world because it is still new.
- **The reader will never know the truth.** It's only ever insinuated.

**Before a pruning (from the first-story idea)**
- Warning signs are random, like Domination's effects, and cosmic in scale. The sky falls and kills people. Lightning carves new landscapes, like lightning-shaped mountains.

**Proposed**
- Near a pruner, magic behaves the way it does in a mature world: it cooperates. To people who have only known magic that bites, the future looks like a curse.
- The "warnings" are really byproducts, like a pressure drop before a storm. Characters read them as warnings, which feeds the lich's belief that the pruners are aware and hostile.
- Ways to insinuate without confirming: unreliable sources, cultures with contradictory explanations, physical details that only make sense in hindsight, and the lich's own wrong theories in the book.

## Magic: Domination vs. Influence

**Settled**
- About one in two or three people are born with magic.
- **Domination** is the easy way. It acts directly on its object and uses magic's energy to force obedience. Its side effects are unexpected, sometimes scary, but manageable, and they hit the environment (a nearby tree becomes a fountain of gravel when someone mends metal).
- **Influence** is the natural state of magic. It asks the subject to obey, then gives it the power to obey. It is extremely painful and difficult, physically and mentally, and its side effects fall only on the caster.
- People who tried Influence didn't die from the pain. They stopped because it was hard.
- Influence has a ceiling. Past it, magic starts to align with the caster's wishes. Many people have reached it. It changes them, and they can't go back. If they go back to Domination, *then* they die.
- No one realized that after going back to Domination, they could have turned back to Influence, and that would have saved them.
- The person who matures the world must turn to Influence, stick to it, turn back to Domination, reject it, and return to Influence, over and over. **That person is the lich, without realizing it.**

**In the draft so far**
- Matthis's "Please" in chapter 18: Domination destroys the cages (thread, vines, red smoke, rotting leaves), while persuasion leaves them whole and costs him (gray skin, black veins, blood from the ear).
- The Collegium's Tenets of Magic are codification in action.

## Society and the reset cycle

**Settled**
- How people treat magic is the whole crux.
- When people try to control the chaos at scale, by ritualizing, codifying, or making magic a religion, the chaos is controlled but the world goes stale and stagnant, and the pruners reset it.
- The lich has watched this many times.
- He thinks chaos is the way to stave off the reset. **The actual answer is maturity:** one person who chooses Influence over control.
- The world is still infantile. If the lich understood that, he would find a way to mature it.

**Proposed**
- The loop: Domination's side effects accumulate → fear → control → stagnation → pruning.
- "Pruners" hints at the truth he's missing. Pruning isn't destruction, it's maintenance so something grows.
- Mercy becoming the Collegium's registrar (non-canon draft 021) is this whole failure in miniature: grief answered by codification.

## Series structure

**Settled**
- A Cosmere-style shared universe. Every book includes the lich somehow, and each one points toward the same thing.
- Novels, not short stories. A trilogy or quartet.
- The first story is his becoming the lich. It's a horror story: omens, he and his friends trying to figure out what's happening, the pruners arriving, his friends melting into dust, him attacking, becoming the lich, and the world resetting.
- The reader doesn't need to know he survived. That's a big reveal later.

**Proposed**
- The opening ends at the moment he attacks, so readers assume he died.
- Plant one clue in that first story: he survives something that kills everyone around him, and the story moves on too quickly for readers to dwell on it.
- Bookends: the first story is his act of force, the last is his act of asking. His POV is saved for the end.
